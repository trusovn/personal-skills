# Try clearer explanations before changing the guidance

We agreed that plans, tasks, and reports should explain their practical meaning
before presenting workflow details. Each fact should have one authoritative
home, with links from other documents. The next step is to apply these choices
to planning guidance and task explanations.

This is the T-001 pilot result for [the project plan](project-plan.md#5-implementation-tasks).
Authority: the owner's 2026-10-21 request to start the plan tasks, followed by
the step-by-step discussion recorded below. This record contains the examples
and feedback; it does not replace the plan or an independent review.

## Owner feedback and resulting choices

1. The owner confirmed the issue: workflow details obscure the practical
   meaning of the work (“sounds right”).
2. The owner agreed to the task explanation and added that the change must
   account for duplicated information across documents.
3. The owner approved the ownership approach and report example with one
   correction: “No skill guidance or templates have changed yet” need not be
   repeated most of the time.

Use short contextual repetition only where a reader needs it. Keep requirements,
observations, and decisions with their owning records. Mention unchanged areas
only when they affect interpretation, scope, or the next action. Retain material
uncertainty and evidence limits; omit routine absence-of-change statements.

The owner clarity checkpoint is satisfied by this explicit feedback and
approval of the corrected approach. No separate comprehension exercise or fresh
reader trial was performed; neither is claimed as evidence here.
